Fermented Foods That Are Actually Worth Eating for Gut Health

SO

Sam Okafor

Updated June 2026 · 7 min read
⚠ Placeholder content — products and prices need real verification before publishing.

The fermented food aisle is full of products claiming microbiome benefits. The critical distinction is between live fermented foods and pasteurised imitations. Heat-treated kefir, vinegar-pickled sauerkraut, and filtered kombucha contain no live cultures whatsoever. Here's what's actually worth buying.

01

Biotiful Dairy Organic Kefir Drink

Live Cultured Kefir
CFU10B+ per serving
TypeMilk Kefir
FatFull-Fat

Biotiful is one of the few UK kefir brands that produces a genuinely live product — not pasteurised after fermentation. A 2021 Stanford study found that fermented foods including kefir were more effective at increasing microbiome diversity than a high-fibre diet alone. The full-fat version is preferable — fat acts as a partial buffer against stomach acid, helping more organisms reach the small intestine alive. Widely available in UK supermarkets.

Pros

  • Genuinely live — not pasteurised after fermentation
  • 10B+ CFU per serving — comparable to a quality probiotic capsule
  • Available in major UK supermarkets without specialist ordering

Cons

  • Not suitable for those with dairy intolerance
  • Short shelf life — needs consuming within days of opening

Vadasz Raw & Unpasteurised Kimchi

Live Fermented Kimchi
ProcessRaw, Unpasteurised
Key StrainL. kimchii
Serving2 tbsp daily

Vadasz is one of the most widely available raw kimchi brands in UK supermarkets. The unpasteurised process preserves Lactobacillus kimchii and related strains that are specific to the brassica fermentation environment — a genuinely distinct microbial profile from dairy-based fermented products. Combining kefir and kimchi in the same daily routine introduces two very different microbial populations, which is more beneficial than consuming more of the same type.

Pros

  • Distinct microbial profile to dairy kefir — different strains, different benefits
  • Raw and unpasteurised — live cultures confirmed on label
  • Available in Waitrose, Ocado, and most health food retailers

Cons

  • Strong flavour — not universally enjoyed
  • Needs refrigerating; continues to ferment and intensify in flavour over time

Remedy Kombucha Original

Long-Aged Live Kombucha
Sugar0g per can
ProcessLong-Aged
LiveYes

Most commercial kombucha is filtered and sweetened to the point of having minimal live culture content. Remedy's long-aged brewing process consumes all residual sugar during fermentation, resulting in 0g sugar per can while maintaining a genuinely live culture population. The organic acids produced during extended fermentation — acetic acid, lactic acid — also have their own evidence for supporting the gut environment. One of the few kombucha products worth the premium.

Pros

  • 0g sugar — long fermentation process consumes all residual sugar
  • Live cultures confirmed — not filtered post-fermentation
  • Organic acids provide additional gut environment benefits

Cons
